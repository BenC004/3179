01

The national picture

A steady total.
Two different directions.

Begin with national enrolment. Then compare primary and secondary education. Their opposing changes almost cancel each other out.

The question

What does the national total conceal?

Combined enrolment fell by 4,537 students between 2017 and 2025. Primary enrolment grew while secondary enrolment declined.

A balance of opposing changes

Primary enrolment rose 2.4% between 2017 and 2025. Secondary enrolment fell 3.2% over this period. These opposing changes left combined enrolment almost unchanged.

What this tells us The national total hides differences between school stages.

02

State differences

One country.
Uneven change.

The same balancing effect appears across states. Compare student gains and losses first. Then distinguish percentage growth from additional student numbers.

The question

Where is enrolment growing, and where is it declining?

Selangor added 51,626 students between 2017 and 2025. Sarawak lost 43,214 students during this period. Large state changes resulted in a small national decline.

Gains and losses offset one another

Each bar adds another state’s change. Increases are progressively offset by decreases. The final national change is −4,537 students.

Which state grew fastest? Which added the most students?

Growth depends on the starting point

Putrajaya’s enrolment rose 71.6% between 2017 and 2025. Selangor’s enrolment rose 5.8% during this period. A smaller starting population can produce larger percentage changes.

The largest concentration remains in Selangor

Selangor held 19.5% of national enrolment in 2025. That represents almost one in five students. Circle area represents student numbers.

The endpoints show the overall change. The annual pattern shows when it happened.

A decline followed by partial recovery

Perlis recorded a 24.7% fall in 2018. Its 2025 enrolment remained 18.0% below 2017. The colour scale is capped at ±5%. Larger declines share the darkest shade. Hover for exact values.

What this tells us Read student counts, percentage changes and timing together. Each answers a different question.

03

The next generation

Today’s students.
Tomorrow’s school demand.

Who attends school today? How is the younger population changing? Student composition describes the present. Births and age groups provide future context.

The question

What demographic changes accompany enrolment trends?

Boys and girls have nearly equal enrolment shares. Births fell 18.4% between 2017 and 2024. These measures describe different generations.

A nearly balanced student population

Girls represented 48.7% of primary enrolment nationally in 2025. They represented 49.4% of secondary enrolment. Waffle cells use rounded shares. Hover for exact values.

Declines varied across states

Every state recorded fewer births in 2024 than 2017. Putrajaya’s birth count fell 38.5%. Malaysia’s total fell 18.4%, reaching 414,918 births. These percentages compare changes, rather than population sizes. Select a state to outline its row.

Compare the youngest generation with older age groups.

The youngest cohort became smaller

Malaysia’s 0–4 population numbered about 2.61 million in 2017. It fell to about 2.26 million in 2025. Primary enrolment increased over this period. These measures describe different age groups.

Do population and enrolment move together across states? Compare their growth profiles.

Follow one state across three measures

Select a state to follow its profile. Compare child population growth with enrolment growth. The axes compare measures, not years. Ages 5–14 do not exactly match either school stage.

What this tells us Demographic change provides context for enrolment. The measures do not move together everywhere.

04

Schools & teachers

Changing numbers.
Different local needs.

Student counts describe only part of provision. Compare schools, teachers and district staffing ratios. Each measure reveals a different aspect.

The question

How does provision differ across places?

Sarawak had more government schools than Selangor in 2025. Its average enrolment per school was much lower. School counts and school sizes reveal different patterns.

More schools, fewer students per school

Sarawak had 1,460 schools in 2025. It averaged approximately 277 students per school. Selangor had 945 schools. It averaged approximately 995 students per school. These averages do not measure classroom size.

Schools describe one aspect of provision. Teacher numbers provide another perspective.

Compare teacher growth with student growth

National primary teacher numbers rose 2.8% from 2017 to 2025. Primary enrolment rose 2.4% over this period. Secondary teacher numbers fell 0.6%. Secondary enrolment fell 3.2%. Use the controls to compare places and school stages.

State summaries can hide differences among districts.

Look inside each state

Compare primary district ratios in 2025. Selangor’s median was 15.5 students per teacher. Sarawak’s median was 6.8. Malaysia’s overall ratio was 11.6. Boxes show the middle half of districts. These ratios do not directly measure class size.

What this tells us National and state averages provide starting points. District comparisons reveal differences hidden by these totals.
