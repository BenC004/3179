Malaysia's Education Trends

Student enrolment, demographics, schools and teachers

Malaysia's student numbers look stable nationally, but how are they changing across states?

This visualisation explores enrolment from 2017 to 2025, demographic patterns and the provision of schools and teachers.

Part 1 — Enrolment trends

How have student numbers changed over time?

A stable national total can conceal different trends in primary and secondary enrolment.

National context

Malaysia's primary enrolment increased by 2.4% from 2017 to 2025, while secondary enrolment fell by 3.2%. Together, they changed by only −0.09%.

Part 2 — State differences

Where are enrolments growing or declining?

Compare state contributions, percentage changes, student concentration and annual enrolment patterns.

Growth and decline offset one another

Selangor added 51,626 students between 2017 and 2025. Sarawak lost 43,214. The national net change was much smaller: a decline of 4,537 students.

A large percentage from a small base

Putrajaya's combined enrolment grew by 71.6%, compared with 5.8% in Selangor. Percentage growth and the number of additional students answer different questions.

One in five students

Selangor accounted for approximately 19.5% of Malaysia's government primary and secondary enrolment in 2025—the largest share among states and territories.

Timing matters

Perlis recorded a 24.7% fall in combined enrolment in 2018. Later gains left its 2025 total 18.0% below 2017. The colour cap means the 2018 decline looks similar to smaller declines.

Part 3 — Demographics

How do gender and demographic patterns differ?

Student composition, the young population and births provide context for current enrolment and future school demand.

National context

In Malaysia in 2025, girls represented 48.7% of primary students and 49.4% of secondary students. Primary education accounted for 57.1% of combined enrolment.

National context

Malaysia recorded 414,918 births in 2024, 18.4% fewer than in 2017. Births rebounded in 2023 before falling again in 2024.

Read across each profile

Each connected line represents one state across three different growth measures. It is not a timeline. Differences between the axes show how population and enrolment changes diverge.

National context

Malaysia's population aged 0–4 fell from about 2.61 million in 2017 to 2.26 million in 2025. The youngest cohort became smaller even as primary enrolment increased.

Part 4 — Schools and teachers

Are schools and teachers keeping pace?

Compare school provision with student numbers, examine teacher growth and explore staffing differences across districts.

More schools does not mean more students per school

In 2025, Sarawak had 1,460 government primary and secondary schools, averaging about 277 students per school. Selangor had 945, averaging about 995. These are school-level averages, not classroom sizes.

National context

From 2017 to 2025, primary teacher numbers rose by 2.8%, close to enrolment growth of 2.4%. Secondary teacher numbers fell by 0.6%, compared with a 3.2% enrolment decline.

Within-state differences

For primary education in 2025, Selangor's median district ratio was about 15.5 students per teacher, compared with 6.8 in Sarawak. Malaysia's overall ratio was 11.6. Ratios do not directly measure class size.
